Why this actually matters

A hardcoded credential doesn't stay hidden forever — and the cost of one getting out is rarely small.

Without scanning first

  • A pasted API key can be used by anyone who sees that conversation — including logs, screenshots, or shared chat histories
  • Cloud credentials (AWS, GCP, Azure) can rack up real charges in someone else's hands within hours
  • A leaked database password is an open door to every record inside it
  • Once shared, a secret can't be "un-shared" — rotating it after the fact is the only fix, and only if you notice in time

With Credential Scrubber

  • Every hardcoded secret is found and masked before it ever leaves your machine
  • Works the same whether you're pasting into an AI chat, a support ticket, or a public repo
  • Nothing is uploaded, logged, or stored anywhere outside your own device
  • Free, open-source, and fast enough to run before every share — not just once a year

Does sanitized code still work for an AI coding agent?

Masking a secret is only useful if the AI can still do its job without it. So we ran a controlled experiment: the same AI coding agent, given the same files, once with the real values and once with the sanitized version — then scored by hand to see if anything was lost.

What we found

Across 48 matched comparisons, most of what we measured held up. Correctness and consistency were essentially unchanged between the two versions. Usefulness showed a statistically distinguishable difference in a minority of cases — 7 of 48 pairs — concentrated in two kinds of tasks (generating tests and suggesting refactors), where the sanitized version scored lower. Completeness showed a similar, smaller pattern that didn't clear our significance threshold. We also ran a control where the "sanitized" file was byte-identical to the original — and one of its pairs still changed, which tells us some of this variation comes from the AI itself, not just from sanitization. Across all 96 responses, we found zero cases of the AI merging two different secrets together, splitting one secret into two, reconstructing a real value, or generating code that wouldn't run because of a placeholder.

What we didn't test

This is one evaluation pass, on one AI model, over 9 hand-picked files — not a large study, and not a claim that this works the same way for every model, every codebase, or every kind of secret. It doesn't test resistance to a malicious AI agent, and it doesn't prove sanitization caused every difference we saw — see the control above. We're publishing the full methodology and every score so you can check our work.

How it keeps you safe

  • Nothing leaves your device. Whether you use the browser version or the desktop app, all scanning happens locally. No server ever sees your code.
  • Originals are never touched. You always get a separate, cleaned copy — your real files are never modified or deleted.
  • Open source. The full detection logic is public — you don't have to trust a black box.
  • If it finds a real secret, rotate it. Masking a credential for sharing doesn't undo the fact that it existed in your code. Treat any real finding as compromised.
